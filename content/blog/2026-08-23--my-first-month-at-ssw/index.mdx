It's pretty hard to believe that my internship at SSW has already surpassed a month, it feels like it was only yesterday that I was walking in and meeting the Newcastle team. As much work as it has been, I couldn't be more grateful for not only the experience I've gained, but also all the people that I'm happy to call my colleagues!

To say it has been a challenge would be a massive understatement. From code review and learning how to communicate professionally with clients, to understanding the complexities (and simplicities) that come with writing Enterprise grade software. Every single day has presented a new challenge and opportunity to learn and grow, I am super excited to see what is in store for my next month!

Coming from University and [Personal Projects](https://github.com/harley-j) I already went in with a decent amount of software knowledge, with that said I've been humbled everyday that I've stepped into office. Every code review or sprint retro has given me lessons that are worth their weight in gold. Before starting this internship I assumed that enterprise software was very complex (which it is), but I'm starting to think that the most complex part of writing commerical software is actually knowing when to keep things simple, it is incredibly easy to overengineer something but it is much harder to engineer something simple that does exactly what it is meant to do efficiently.

For my auto heads, a good example might be something like the [Bugatti W16 Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugatti_W16_engine), it's an insanely engineered engine. It combines two v8 engines into a 16 cylinder configuration — that is incredibly cool, but you wouldn't put it in a Toyota Corolla.

![](/Volkswagen_W16.jpg)

One of the most beautiful things about SSW is that it's a multi-fauceted organisation, we're not just devs but we are also consultants and sales-people. We constantly deal with massive clients and talking to managers and stakeholders at other companies brings its own challenges.

It's very easy to fall into the trap of showing a Product Owner what you've been working on and over explain the technical details, the reality is that most Product Owners and Stakeholders (usually a client) are more focused on what value your work has brought — not necessarily why you decided to use a HashSet for a permissions system.

Learning when to explain the **"how"** and when to explain the **“why”** is key in keeping the client happy with the value of the work that you have provided to them, a great Engineer at SSW isn't just someone that can build but also somebody that can explain their solution clearly to the person or stakeholder that depends on it.

## What's next?

At SSW I have been given the opportunity to contribute to an amazing product that I personally use — [TinaCMS ](https://tina.io)and [TinaCloud](https://app.tina.io). It is incredibly rewarding working on a product that many sites that I have visited thousands of times have been using in the background for years (namely [the Unity Game Engine](https://unity.com) documentation)

I'm incredibly excited to learn more about each respective codebase and help the team continue moving the product forward.

## Closing

Thank you so so much to everyone at SSW who has made my first month an incredible experience and learning opportunity. From taking the time to answer all my endless questions, to genuinely mentoring me and pushing me in the best possible direction, I've felt incredibly welcomed from day one.

I'm extremely grateful for the opportunity that this internship has given me, I'm also extremely grateful and humbled to be surrounding by some of the nicest and most professional people that I have ever met — seriously, the atmosphere at SSW is incredible. This first month has taught me more than I ever could've asked for, not just about Software Development but also perseverance, and how OK it is to make mistakes and ask questions.

I'm excited to see what my next couple of months bring me at SSW, stay tuned for more 👀
